Glossary

Owners' Earnings
The real cash profit left for shareholders once the true cost of stock-based compensation is backed out, a more honest number than accounting net income.
Cash Cost of SBC
What paying employees in stock actually costs existing shareholders in cash terms, once you count buybacks used to offset dilution and taxes withheld on vesting stock. Not the smaller non-cash number on the income statement.
IV8 / IV10 / IV12 / IV15
The fair value of a stock at four different required-return bars. IV15 is the strictest: the most you could pay today and still expect a 15% annualized return over 15 years. IV8 is the loosest, most forgiving estimate.
P/IV
Current price divided by intrinsic value at your chosen hurdle. Below 1.0x means the stock is trading under fair value (undervalued); above 1.0x means it's trading over fair value (overvalued).
Fat Pitch
A borrowed baseball term for a rare, unusually attractive opportunity: a good business trading well below its intrinsic value, worth swinging at.
Perpetuity Leg
One of two valuation estimates this tool blends: the "forever" case, assuming cash flow keeps growing at the same rate indefinitely.
Year-15 Leg
The second estimate: a more conservative case that only projects 15 years out, then assumes the business is valued at a set earnings multiple at that point instead of growing forever.
Net Cash Per Share
Cash and short-term investments minus total debt, divided by shares outstanding. Added on top of the discounted owners'-earnings value at full, undiscounted value, since it's cash already on the balance sheet rather than a future projection. Negative for companies carrying more debt than cash.
Terminal Multiple
The earnings multiple assumed for the Year-15 leg's ending value: essentially "what P/E ratio might this business still command in 15 years," once its growth has matured.
Dilution Rate
How fast the total share count grows or shrinks each year. A positive rate means more shares get created than bought back, so each existing share represents a slightly smaller slice of the company over time.
Discount / Hurdle Rate
The annual return you require to make an investment worth it. A higher hurdle demands a lower purchase price today. It's the "d" behind IV8 through IV15.
